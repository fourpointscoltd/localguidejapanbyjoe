Is there an Apple Watch band with a Japanese design available?

If you’re wandering around Japan and want an Apple Watch band that feels a bit more local, you’re in luck! One brand I personally trust and admire is Tsuchiya Kaban. They’re famous for their leather bags, but recently they’ve crafted some Apple Watch bands too. The cool thing? Their style is distinctly Japanese—simple, high-quality leather with a subtle elegance you often find in traditional Japanese craftsmanship.

Tsuchiya Kaban isn’t everywhere, but they have about 10 shops scattered around Japan, mainly in well-known cities. So, if you’re visiting places like Tokyo or Kyoto, you can probably swing by a store without too much trouble.

Here’s a bit more to help you decide if it’s worth the detour and what to expect:

> Where can I find Tsuchiya Kaban stores in major cities?

Tsuchiya Kaban has stores in popular areas like Tokyo’s Ginza and Shibuya, as well as Kyoto’s downtown. These locations are often near other shopping spots, so you can combine your visit with sightseeing or grabbing a coffee. The stores themselves are cozy and showcase not just Apple Watch bands but also their signature leather goods. It’s a nice way to get a feel for Japanese leather craftsmanship. If you’re in Tokyo, the Ginza store is especially convenient and easy to reach by subway.

> How much do these Apple Watch bands cost?

Their bands are made from quality leather, so expect to pay a bit more than generic options. Prices hover around ¥15,000 to ¥20,000 (about $110 to $150 USD). It’s not cheap, but you’re paying for craftsmanship and materials that age beautifully. Plus, Japanese leather products tend to last a long time if you take care of them. From my experience, it’s more of an investment piece than a souvenir you toss after a trip.

> Are these bands suitable for daily wear?

Absolutely! Many Japanese people value durability and understated style in everyday items, and Tsuchiya Kaban’s bands fit just that. I’ve seen young and older folks alike sporting them with their Apple Watches, whether at work or casual outings. The leather softens over time, molding to your wrist, which adds to the charm. Just avoid getting them wet too often since leather can be sensitive.

> Can tourists realistically buy these bands and bring them home?

Yes, you can buy them tax-free if you show your passport, which makes the price a bit more attractive. The stores are welcoming to foreigners, and staff are used to explaining products in English. Just keep in mind the size of your Apple Watch when choosing a band. They usually have models for the common sizes, but it’s good to confirm beforehand. If you’re unsure or can’t visit a store, their online shop sometimes ships internationally, but it’s best to check current policies.

> How do Japanese people choose their Apple Watch bands?

While some follow trends, many Japanese people prefer simplicity and quality over flashy designs. A leather band like Tsuchiya Kaban’s fits well with that mindset. It’s common to have a couple of bands for different occasions—one casual, one more formal. Switching bands is easy with Apple Watch, and many treat it like an accessory that complements their outfit. So, picking a local brand’s band is a subtle way to connect with Japanese style.

Overall, if you want a genuine Japanese touch for your Apple Watch, Tsuchiya Kaban offers something authentic and durable. It’s not just about slapping a souvenir on your wrist but owning a piece of local craftsmanship that grows with you. When I picked up my own band, I enjoyed chatting with the staff about their leather techniques and felt like I brought home a little story from Japan, not just a product.
