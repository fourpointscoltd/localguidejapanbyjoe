> "Are taxes included in the displayed price when it comes to price tags in stores?"

**Yes!** In Japan, the price you see is the price you pay. No surprises at the register!

---

## How Pricing Works

> "So the price tag is the final price?"

That's right. If a price tag says **¥1,000,** you pay **¥1,000**. Tax is already included.

**The consumption tax in Japan:**
- **10%** for most items
- **8%** for food and takeout items

This 8% rate applies to groceries, convenience store food, and takeout meals.

---

## One Exception: Service Charges

> "Are there any extra fees I should know about?"

At some restaurants, there may be:
- **Service charges** (usually 10%)
- **Table charges** (otoshi) at izakayas

These should be listed on the menu or at the entrance. But for regular shopping? The price tag is final.

---

## What Japanese People Actually Do

Most locals:
- Trust the price tag completely
- Don't calculate tax separately
- Know that "tax included" is the law since 2021

---

## Extra Travel Tips

1. **No tipping** – Service is included, no tips expected

2. **Receipts show tax** – You can see the breakdown if curious

3. **Duty-free shopping** – Some stores offer tax-free for tourists (look for "Tax Free" signs)

4. **Convenience stores** – Always tax included
