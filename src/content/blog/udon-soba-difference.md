> "What's the difference between udon and soba?"

**Both are traditional Japanese noodles!** But they're very different.

---

## The Basic Difference

> "How are they different?"

| Feature | Udon | Soba |
|---------|------|------|
| Made from | Wheat flour | Buckwheat flour |
| Texture | Thick, chewy | Thin, slightly firm |
| Color | White | Brown/gray |
| Taste | Mild, neutral | Nutty, earthy |

---

## Udon

> "Tell me more about udon."

**Characteristics:**
- Thick white noodles
- Very chewy texture
- Served hot or cold
- Mild flavor absorbs broth

**Popular styles:**
- **Sanuki udon** (Kagawa) – Most famous
- **Kitsune udon** – With sweet fried tofu
- **Tempura udon** – With tempura on top
- **Curry udon** – In curry broth

---

## Soba

> "What about soba?"

**Characteristics:**
- Thin brown noodles
- Made from buckwheat
- Nutty flavor
- Often served cold with dipping sauce

**Popular styles:**
- **Zaru soba** – Cold with dipping sauce
- **Kake soba** – Hot in broth
- **Tempura soba** – With tempura
- **Toshikoshi soba** – New Year's tradition

---

## Hot or Cold?

> "When do you eat them hot or cold?"

**Hot versions:**
- Winter
- In broth (tsuyu)
- Comforting meal

**Cold versions:**
- Summer
- Dipped in sauce
- Refreshing option

Both work year-round!

---

## What Japanese People Actually Do

Most locals:
- Have personal preferences
- Eat soba on New Year's Eve (tradition)
- Know regional specialties
- Slurp noodles loudly (it's normal!)

---

## Extra Travel Tips

1. **Slurping is OK** – Actually expected!

2. **Standing noodle shops** – Quick, cheap, delicious

3. **Regional varieties** – Each area has specialties

4. **Soba tea** – Try the hot water used to boil soba (sobayu)

5. **Allergies** – Buckwheat allergies exist, be careful with soba
