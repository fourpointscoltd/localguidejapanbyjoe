Japan's train system can feel like a maze at first because it's run by different companies depending on where you are and what lines you take. But don't worry - it's actually very user-friendly once you understand the basics!

## Why It Seems Complicated

- **Multiple operators** - JR, Metro, private railways all in one city
- **Different ticket types** - But IC cards solve this!
- **Huge stations** - Some have 20+ exits

## My Top Tips for Beginners

### 1. Get a Suica or Pasmo Card
This is the **#1 tip**. These IC cards work on virtually ALL trains, buses, and even convenience stores. No need to figure out fares!

### 2. Use Google Maps
It shows:
- Real-time train schedules
- Platform numbers
- Walking directions inside stations
- Total fare

### 3. Follow the Colors
Each train line has a color code. Follow the colored signs and you won't get lost.

### 4. Know Key Phrases
- **出口 (deguchi)** = Exit
- **改札 (kaisatsu)** = Ticket gate
- **乗り換え (norikae)** = Transfer

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

1. **Don't rush** - Trains run every 2-5 minutes in Tokyo
2. **Stand on the correct side** of escalators (left in Tokyo, right in Osaka)
3. **Keep your IC card accessible** - You'll tap it frequently
4. **Don't eat on trains** - It's considered rude

## The Truth

After a day or two, you'll feel like a pro! The system is incredibly punctual, clean, and safe. Trust it!
