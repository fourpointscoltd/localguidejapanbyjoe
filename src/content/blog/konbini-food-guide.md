> "Is convenience store food in Japan actually good? People say it's amazing."

**Yes, it's true!** Japanese convenience store food is much better than other countries. Let me explain.

---

## Why It's So Good

> "What makes it different?"

**The reasons:**
- Fresh food delivered multiple times daily
- High competition between chains
- Japanese standards for quality
- Real ingredients, not just processed food
- Constant new products

---

## Best Things to Try

> "What should I get?"

**Must-try items:**
- **Onigiri** – Rice balls with various fillings (¥100-200)
- **Egg sandwich** – Famously fluffy and delicious
- **Karaage** – Fried chicken, especially Famichiki
- **Nikuman** – Steamed meat buns (winter)
- **Oden** – Hot pot items (winter)
- **Desserts** – Pudding, cheesecake, cream puffs

---

## The Big Three Chains

> "Which convenience store is best?"

**7-Eleven**
- Best rice balls and hot food
- ATMs that work with foreign cards

**Lawson**
- Famous for Karaage-kun (fried chicken)
- Good desserts

**FamilyMart**
- Great Famichiki fried chicken
- Good variety

All three are good – try them all!

---

## Budget Travel Tip

> "Can I eat cheap at konbini?"

**Yes!**
- Breakfast: Onigiri + coffee = ¥300 ($2)
- Lunch: Bento box = ¥400-600 ($3-4)
- Late night: Discounted items with stickers

Great way to save money while traveling!

---

## What Japanese People Actually Do

Most locals:
- Use konbini for quick meals regularly
- Know which chain has the best specific items
- Look for seasonal limited items
- Check for discount stickers in evenings

---

## Extra Travel Tips

1. **Hot food counter** – Ask staff to heat items

2. **Microwave available** – Free to use in store

3. **Eating area** – Some stores have seating

4. **Seasonal items** – Change regularly, try new things

5. **Late-night discounts** – 20-50% off items near expiry
