> "Where is the best place in Tokyo to get chopsticks customized with your name engraved on them?"

Custom-engraved chopsticks are one of the **best souvenirs** you can bring home from Japan. They're personal, beautiful, and uniquely Japanese. Here are the top spots in Tokyo:

## 1. Ginza Natsuno (銀座 夏野) — Ginza

The most famous chopstick specialty shop in Japan.

- **Customization:** Name engraving in kanji, katakana, or Roman letters
- **Processing time:** Usually same day, within 30 minutes to an hour
- **Price:** ~¥1,000–¥3,000 per pair, engraving ~¥500 extra
- **Location:** Near Ginza Station, very easy to access
- **Why go here:** Massive selection, from everyday chopsticks to luxury gift sets

## 2. Hashicho (箸長) — Asakusa

Perfect if you're already exploring Sensoji Temple.

- **Customization:** Engraving available, great selection of classic and modern designs
- **Processing time:** Usually done on the spot
- **Location:** Near Nakamise Shopping Street
- **Why go here:** Traditional atmosphere, easy to combine with sightseeing

## 3. Tokyu Hands — Shibuya, Ikebukuro, etc.

The big department store chain sometimes offers chopstick engraving.

- **Customization:** Some locations have engraving stations, especially during holiday seasons
- **Note:** Not always guaranteed — ask at the gift section or information counter
- **Why go here:** Convenient if you're already shopping for other things

## 4. Nihonbashi Nishikawa — Near Tokyo Station

A long-established specialty chopstick store.

- **Customization:** High-end options with personalized engraving
- **Presentation:** Beautiful boxed sets — perfect for gifts
- **Why go here:** Premium quality, great for special occasions

## Engraving Options

You can usually choose from:

- **Kanji** — Chinese characters with Japanese meaning (most popular with visitors)
- **Katakana** — Japanese phonetic script for foreign names
- **Roman letters** — your name in English alphabet

**My recommendation:** Get your name in **katakana**. It's uniquely Japanese and shows your name as Japanese people would write it.
