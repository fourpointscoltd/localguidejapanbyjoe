> "How do I buy bullet train (Shinkansen) tickets from Osaka to Tokyo? And should I fly instead?"

Let me save you some research: **the Shinkansen is definitely the best way to travel between Osaka and Tokyo.** Flying isn't really practical because of airport transfers and security lines—by the time you factor everything in, the train is faster and more convenient. Let me show you exactly how to buy tickets.

---

## Why Shinkansen Over Flying?

> "Wouldn't flying be faster?"

On paper, a flight is about 1 hour vs. the Shinkansen's 2.5 hours. But here's the reality:

| Factor | Shinkansen | Flight |
|--------|------------|--------|
| **Station to station** | 2h 15min - 2h 30min | N/A |
| **Airport transfers** | N/A | 1-2 hours each way |
| **Check-in/security** | Walk in, board | 30-60 min minimum |
| **Total time** | ~2.5 hours | ~4-5 hours |
| **City center to city center** | Yes | No (airports are outside cities) |
| **Frequency** | Every 10-15 min | Limited daily flights |

The Shinkansen departs from the city center and arrives in the city center. No taxis, no shuttles, no security lines. Just show up and go!

## How to Buy Tickets

> "What are my options for buying tickets?"

You have several ways to buy Shinkansen tickets:

### 1. At the Station (Easiest for Beginners)
**Where:** JR Ticket Office (みどりの窓口 / Midori no Madoguchi) or ticket machines

**How:**
1. Go to any major JR station (Osaka, Shin-Osaka, Tokyo, etc.)
2. Visit the green ticket window or use the ticket machines
3. Tell the staff: "Shin-Osaka to Tokyo, [date and time]"
4. Pay and receive your ticket

**Pros:** Staff can help you, pay in person
**Cons:** May have lines, limited English at some stations

### 2. Online (Recommended!)
**Where:** SmartEX app or JR-West online booking

**How:**
1. Download the **SmartEX** app (English available)
2. Create an account
3. Search for your route and time
4. Pay with credit card
5. Either use your phone as a ticket OR collect at station

**Pros:** Book anytime, sometimes discounts, no lines
**Cons:** Need internet connection and credit card

### 3. JR Pass
**Where:** Order before arriving in Japan

**How:**
1. Buy JR Pass online before your trip
2. Activate at a JR office when you arrive
3. Reserve seats at any JR ticket office (free with pass)

**Pros:** Unlimited rides if you're traveling a lot
**Cons:** Expensive if you're only doing Tokyo-Osaka once

## Ticket Prices

> "How much does it cost?"

**Tokyo (Tokyo Station) ↔ Osaka (Shin-Osaka Station):**

| Train Type | Reserved Seat | Unreserved |
|------------|--------------|------------|
| **Nozomi** (Fastest) | ¥13,870 (~$103 USD) | ¥13,320 (~$99 USD) |
| **Hikari** | ¥13,320 (~$99 USD) | ¥12,870 (~$95 USD) |
| **Kodama** (Slowest) | ¥13,320 (~$99 USD) | ¥12,870 (~$95 USD) |

**Note:** Nozomi is not covered by JR Pass. Hikari and Kodama are.

**Travel time:**
- Nozomi: 2 hours 15 minutes
- Hikari: 2 hours 45 minutes
- Kodama: 3 hours 45 minutes (many stops)

## Reserved vs. Unreserved Seats

> "Should I get reserved seats?"

**Reserved seats (指定席):**
- Guaranteed seat
- Choose your specific seat
- Slightly more expensive (~¥500 more)
- Recommended for peak times

**Unreserved seats (自由席):**
- First-come, first-served
- Any available seat in unreserved cars
- Slightly cheaper
- May have to stand if crowded

**My recommendation:** During holidays (Golden Week, Obon, New Year), **always reserve**. During normal times, unreserved is usually fine—trains run every 10-15 minutes, so if one is full, just catch the next.

## What Japanese People Actually Do

Most locals:
- Buy tickets at station machines (it's quick once you know how)
- Don't reserve for short trips unless it's peak season
- Use SmartEX app for convenience
- Take Nozomi because it's fastest (JR Pass holders take Hikari)
- Often book morning trains for business trips

Fun fact: Japanese businesspeople are so used to the Shinkansen that many work on their laptops the entire trip!

## Step-by-Step: Buying at the Machine

> "Can you walk me through the ticket machine?"

**At the station ticket machine:**

1. **Select "Shinkansen"** on the main screen
2. **Choose your destination** (Tokyo or Shin-Osaka)
3. **Select date and time**
4. **Choose "Reserved" or "Unreserved"**
5. **Select number of passengers**
6. **Choose seat preference** (if reserved)
   - Window (窓側/madogawa)
   - Aisle (通路側/tsuurogawa)
7. **Pay** (cash or card)
8. **Collect your ticket(s)**

**Tip:** Most machines have an "English" button in the top corner!

## Pro Tips for Saving Money

> "Any ways to get discounts?"

1. **Early Bird Discount (EX早特)** – Book 21+ days ahead on SmartEX for up to 30% off

2. **Platt Kodama** – ¥10,700 for Kodama (slower but cheaper)

3. **JR Pass** – Only worth it if you're taking 2+ round trips or traveling extensively

4. **Round-trip discount** – Minor savings when booking both ways

5. **Student discount** – If you have a Japanese student ID

## Extra Travel Tips

1. **Shin-Osaka vs. Osaka Station** – The Shinkansen leaves from **Shin-Osaka**, not Osaka Station. They're different! Take a local train between them (5-10 minutes).

2. **Tokyo Station is huge** – Allow 10-15 minutes to find your platform if you're unfamiliar.

3. **Buy an ekiben** – Train bento boxes sold at stations are delicious! Part of the Shinkansen experience.

4. **Sit on the right side** – For views of Mt. Fuji (heading to Tokyo), sit on the right side (seat D or E in reserved).

5. **Keep your ticket** – You'll need it to exit at your destination.

6. **Luggage space** – Behind the last row of each car, there's luggage storage. First come, first served!
