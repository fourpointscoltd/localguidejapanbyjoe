> "Is it safe to walk at night in Tokyo?"

**Yes — Tokyo is one of the safest major cities in the world**, even late at night. I've walked home alone at 2am countless times without a second thought.

## How Safe Is It Really?

- Japan's violent crime rate is **one of the lowest** of any developed country
- **Women walking alone at night** is common and generally safe
- **Children ride trains alone** to school from age 6 — that tells you a lot about the safety culture
- **Lost wallets get returned** with cash still inside — police lost-and-found offices are full of returned items

## Areas to Be Aware Of (Not Dangerous, But...)

| Area | What to Know |
|---|---|
| **Kabukicho (Shinjuku)** | Red light district. Touts may approach you with bar/club invitations. Just say no and keep walking. Not dangerous, but can be annoying |
| **Roppongi late night** | Nightclub area. Some aggressive bar touts. Avoid being pulled into unknown bars (drink prices can be inflated) |
| **Shibuya Center-gai** | Can get rowdy on weekends. Drunk people but not dangerous |

## General Tips

- **Avoid touts** — anyone on the street inviting you into a bar or club, especially in Kabukicho or Roppongi. Politely decline and walk away
- **Don't accept drinks from strangers** — standard advice anywhere in the world
- **Trains stop around midnight** — plan your last train or budget for a taxi/capsule hotel
- **Convenience stores are everywhere** — they're a safe haven if you need directions, a rest, or just to feel comfortable

## What Japanese People Actually Do at Night

- **Walk home from the station** — completely normal, even for women
- **Eat at late-night ramen shops** — some are open until 4-5am
- **Sleep on trains** — you'll see businessmen dozing on the last train. Nobody bothers them
- **Use 24-hour establishments** — manga cafes, karaoke, internet cafes are all safe overnight options
