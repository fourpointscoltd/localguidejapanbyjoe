Honestly, from my experience, there's **really no need to go through a travel agent** when booking hotels in Japan.

## Why DIY Booking Works Well in Japan

I personally stay in Japanese hotels and ryokans about 20 nights a year, and I always book directly through websites. Here's why:

### Easy Booking Platforms
- **Booking.com** - Widest selection, free cancellation options
- **Agoda** - Often cheaper for Asia
- **Japanican** - Good for ryokans
- **Hotels.com** - Loyalty rewards
- **Direct hotel websites** - Sometimes best rates

### Japan is Tourist-Friendly
- **English** widely available on booking sites
- **Clear pricing** - What you see is what you pay
- **Reliable reviews** - Japanese hospitality is consistent
- **Easy communication** - Hotels respond quickly to emails

## When a Travel Agent MIGHT Help

- **First-time travelers** who feel overwhelmed
- **Complex ryokan bookings** with specific meal requirements
- **Peak season** (cherry blossom, autumn leaves) when availability is tight
- **Package deals** that include flights + hotels

## What to Book Yourself

1. **Hotels** - So easy online
2. **JR Pass** - Official website or authorized retailers
3. **Day tours** - Viator, Klook, GetYourGuide
4. **Restaurant reservations** - Tabelog, Pocket Concierge

## Cost Comparison

| Booking Method | Typical Added Cost |
|----------------|-------------------|
| DIY online | ¥0 |
| Travel agent | 10-20% markup |

## My Verdict

Save that agent fee for more sushi! Japan's tourism infrastructure makes independent booking very straightforward.
