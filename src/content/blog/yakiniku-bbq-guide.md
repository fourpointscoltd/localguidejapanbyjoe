> "How does yakiniku work? I've never been to Japanese BBQ."

**You cook your own meat at the table!** It's fun, social, and delicious.

---

## How Yakiniku Works

> "What's the basic system?"

**The setup:**
- Grill built into your table
- Order raw meat and vegetables
- Cook it yourself
- Dip in sauce and eat

**It's interactive** – part of the fun is cooking together!

---

## What to Order

> "What should I try?"

**Popular cuts:**
- **Karubi** – Beef short rib, fatty and flavorful
- **Harami** – Skirt steak, popular choice
- **Tan** – Beef tongue (try it!)
- **Rosu** – Loin, leaner
- **Horumon** – Offal/intestines (for adventurous eaters)

**Also order:**
- Rice
- Vegetables (onions, mushrooms)
- Kimchi
- Salad

---

## How to Cook

> "Any tips for cooking?"

**Cooking tips:**
- Don't overcook – medium is often best
- Flip once
- Cook tongue thin slices quickly
- Fat drips cause flames – move meat if needed
- Use tongs, not chopsticks for raw meat

---

## All-You-Can-Eat

> "What about tabehodai?"

**All-you-can-eat (tabehodai):**
- Fixed price, unlimited meat
- Usually 90 minutes
- Good value for big eaters
- Quality varies – check reviews

**Popular chains:** Gyukaku, Stamina-en

---

## What Japanese People Actually Do

Most locals:
- Go with friends or family
- Order variety of cuts to share
- Know how to cook each cut
- End with rice or noodles

---

## Extra Travel Tips

1. **Ventilation** – Your clothes will smell like BBQ

2. **Drinks** – Beer and yakiniku are best friends

3. **Lunch sets** – Cheaper than dinner

4. **Ask staff** – They'll help if you're unsure

5. **Reservation** – Helpful for popular places
