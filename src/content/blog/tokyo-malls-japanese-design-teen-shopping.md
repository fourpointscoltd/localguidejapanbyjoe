> "Best Tokyo mall with Japanese designers for shopping with 13 yr old?"

### Conclusion

Start your shopping trip at **KITTE near Tokyo Station**. It offers a perfect mix of quality Japanese design and cultural items — not just souvenirs.
If you're looking for more upscale fashion, **Midtown Hibiya** is also a great choice and just a short walk away.

---
### General Information

When shopping in Tokyo, many travelers first think of places like:

- **Don Quijote (Donki)**: A massive discount store known for quirky, cheap items, snacks, souvenirs, and beauty products. Great for quick souvenir shopping, but can be overwhelming and chaotic. 🌀
- **Department stores (Isetan, Mitsukoshi, Takashimaya, etc.)**: Upscale shopping with international luxury brands, food basements, and formal customer service. More traditional, often crowded on weekends.
- **Tokyu Hands / Loft**: Creative lifestyle stores with a mix of stationery, DIY goods, beauty products, and travel items. Fun to browse with teens, especially for everyday-use items.

However, if you're looking for something that reflects **authentic Japanese craftsmanship, unique design, and modern aesthetics**, then places like **KITTE** and **Midtown Hibiya** offer a more curated, calm, and thoughtful shopping experience.

These malls focus on high-quality **Japanese brands**, blending tradition and innovation — perfect for travelers who want more than mass-produced souvenirs.

---
### Recommended Malls for Japanese Design Lovers

#### KITTE (Tokyo Station area)

- Located just outside **Tokyo Station Marunouchi South Exit**
- Features shops that sell goods made with **traditional Japanese craftsmanship**, using local materials and techniques
- Includes **fashion, stationery, crafts, and home goods** — many with beautiful, modern design
- Not a typical souvenir mall — even locals come here for unique gifts and high-quality items
- Great place to **introduce your teen to real Japanese aesthetics**

🧭 *Tip: Go to the rooftop garden for a free view of Tokyo Station’s historic red-brick building.*

#### Midtown Hibiya

- A more luxurious mall, with **high-end fashion**, accessories, and designer items
- Some Japanese brands with elegant, minimalistic design
- Stylish cafés and restaurants for a relaxing shopping break ☕️
- Within **walking distance from KITTE** or Tokyo Station

💡 *Better for browsing higher-end pieces and design-oriented shops. May not be ideal for everyday souvenirs, but great for fashion lovers.*

---
### My Personal Opinion

I used to live near this area and often visited both malls.
**KITTE is my top recommendation** for travelers who want more than just typical tourist goods. It has beautiful design, a calm atmosphere, and many items that reflect Japan’s local culture.
For teens, it's fun to explore different categories — from cute stationery to fashion accessories and art-inspired items.

If you have more time or want to see something fancier, **Midtown Hibiya is a stylish stop** — a bit more adult in feel, but worth visiting.
Both are walkable from Tokyo Station, so you can easily explore both in one afternoon.

---
### Suggested Itinerary

**Start at Tokyo Station**
⬇
Explore **KITTE** – Spend 1–2 hours shopping and enjoying Japanese-made items
⬇
Walk 15–20 minutes to **Midtown Hibiya**
⬇
Relax at a café or enjoy the view of Hibiya Park
⬇
Dinner in the Tokyo Station area or nearby Ginza

---
### Travel Tips

- **Both malls are indoors**, so great for rainy or hot days ☀️☔
- **Tax-free shopping is available** for many items (bring your passport)
- **Try to avoid weekends** if possible, as these malls can get busy
- **Stationery, fashion accessories, and Japanese skincare** are popular with teens
