> "Can you recommend a good and affordable shabu-shabu or sukiyaki place in Ueno or Asakusa?"

**Yes!** The Ueno-Asakusa area has some great hotpot spots. Here are my recommendations:

## My Top Picks

### 1. Nabezo (鍋ぞう) — Ueno
- **Style:** All-you-can-eat shabu-shabu and sukiyaki
- **Price:** ~¥2,500-3,500 per person (90-minute course)
- **Why go:** Great value, English menu, popular with locals
- **Location:** Near Ueno Station (multiple locations in Tokyo)

### 2. Imahan (今半) — Asakusa
- **Style:** Premium sukiyaki with Wagyu beef
- **Price:** ~¥5,000-8,000 per person
- **Why go:** Over 100 years of history, the sukiyaki is exceptional
- **Location:** Near Sensoji Temple

### 3. Kisoji (木曽路) — Multiple locations
- **Style:** Traditional shabu-shabu in private rooms
- **Price:** ~¥3,000-5,000 per person
- **Why go:** Elegant atmosphere, great for special occasions

## Shabu-Shabu vs Sukiyaki: What's the Difference?

| | Shabu-Shabu | Sukiyaki |
|---|---|---|
| **Broth** | Light, clear dashi | Sweet soy-based sauce |
| **Cooking** | Quick swish in hot broth | Simmer in sweet sauce |
| **Dipping** | Ponzu or sesame sauce | Raw egg (optional) |
| **Taste** | Light and refreshing | Rich and sweet |

**My recommendation:** If it's your first time, try **sukiyaki** — the sweet soy flavor is more approachable for most visitors.
