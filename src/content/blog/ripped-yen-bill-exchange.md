> "I have a ripped 5000 yen bill. What should I do with it?"

I'll be honest – **I recommend giving up** on exchanging it. Here's why.

---

## The Official Way to Exchange

> "Can I exchange it somewhere?"

Yes, technically. You can take damaged bills to a **Bank of Japan branch** and they will exchange them for new ones.

**But here's the problem:**
- Bank of Japan branches are in very inconvenient locations
- There are only a few in the whole country
- Going there is a waste of your vacation time

---

## Better Options

> "What else can I do?"

**Option 1: Try using it**
- Some convenience stores might accept it
- Hand it directly to a cashier (not a machine)
- It might work if it's not too damaged

**Option 2: Give it away**
- Give it to someone you meet locally
- Use it as a tip (though tipping isn't common)
- Some people collect damaged currency

**Option 3: Keep it as a souvenir**
- It's still a memory of your trip!

---

## Vending Machines Won't Work

> "Can I use it in a vending machine?"

**No.** Machines are very strict about bill condition. Even slightly wrinkled bills get rejected sometimes.

---

## What Japanese People Actually Do

Most locals:
- Take damaged bills to their regular bank (but tourists can't do this easily)
- Just use them at stores where staff can check them
- Don't worry too much about small damage

---

## Extra Travel Tips

1. **Keep bills flat** – Use a wallet that doesn't fold them

2. **Check before accepting** – Look at change you receive

3. **ATMs give crisp bills** – 7-Eleven ATMs give perfect bills

4. **Japanese currency is tough** – It rarely gets damaged
