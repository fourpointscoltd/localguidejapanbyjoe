> "What's the difference between shrines and temples in Japan?"

**Great question!** Shrines are Shinto, temples are Buddhist. Here's how to tell them apart.

---

## Shrines (Jinja)

> "What are Shinto shrines?"

**Shinto = Japanese native religion**

**How to recognize:**
- Torii gates (often red/orange)
- Name ends in "-jinja" or "-gu"
- Shimenawa (sacred rope)
- Komainu (lion-dog statues)
- No Buddha images

**What you do:**
- Bow, clap twice, bow (for prayers)
- Wash hands at purification fountain
- Buy omamori (charms)

---

## Temples (Tera/Ji)

> "What about Buddhist temples?"

**Buddhism = came from India via China/Korea**

**How to recognize:**
- No torii gates
- Name ends in "-ji," "-in," or "-dera"
- Buddha statues
- Incense burning
- Pagodas (often)
- Cemetery nearby (often)

**What you do:**
- Bow once (no clapping)
- Burn incense
- Prayer beads sometimes used

---

## Quick Comparison

> "How do I remember?"

| Feature | Shrine | Temple |
|---------|--------|--------|
| Religion | Shinto | Buddhist |
| Gate | Torii (red) | Sanmon (large wooden) |
| Prayer | Bow, clap, bow | Bow only |
| Statues | Komainu (lions) | Buddha |
| Name | -jinja, -gu | -ji, -dera |

---

## Can I Visit Both?

> "Is it OK to visit both?"

**Absolutely!** Many Japanese visit both:
- Shrines for New Year
- Temples for funerals
- Both for sightseeing

Japan is very flexible about religion.

---

## What Japanese People Actually Do

Most locals:
- Visit both without thinking too much
- Pray at shrines for good luck
- Visit temples for ancestors
- Don't follow strict religious rules

---

## Extra Travel Tips

1. **Remove shoes** – When entering buildings at both

2. **Photos** – Usually OK outside, ask about inside

3. **Quiet please** – Both are sacred places

4. **Opening hours** – Usually sunrise to sunset

5. **Famous examples** – Meiji Shrine (shrine), Senso-ji (temple)
